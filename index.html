Traveler Questions

Questions every
visitor asks us

Real answers to the things tourists worry about most — before and during their trip.

Is Sri Lanka safe for solo travelers? +
Sri Lanka is generally very safe for solo travelers, including women. The main risks are tourist-targeted scams (not violence), traffic accidents, and occasional petty theft at crowded sites. Our Scam Shield covers the 50 most common tricks in detail. Use PickMe or Uber instead of unmarked taxis, and you'll have a smooth trip.
How much should I budget per day in Sri Lanka? +
Budget travelers: $25–$40/day (guesthouses, local food, buses). Mid-range: $60–$120/day (boutique hotels, some tours, private transport). Luxury: $200–$500+/day. The biggest overspend trap is transport — always use PickMe over street tuk-tuks for airport and city routes. Our Price Oracle has exact breakdowns for every expense.
What are Poya days and how do they affect my trip? +
Poya days are full moon days observed as national holidays. Alcohol is banned across the country — shops, restaurants, and hotels cannot sell it. Some cultural sites have restricted access. They occur monthly. Our itinerary builder automatically flags Poya days in your travel dates so you can plan around them.
Do I need a visa to visit Sri Lanka? +
Most nationalities require an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) which you can apply for online at eta.gov.lk. Cost is typically $35–$50 USD. The process takes minutes. Beware of third-party "visa assistance" websites that charge 3–5x more — use only the official government portal. Some nationalities receive free visas — check with your embassy.
What's the best way to get around Sri Lanka? +
The famous Kandy-to-Ella train is the most scenic train journey in Asia — book seats weeks ahead at 12go.asia or the railway station directly. For cities, use PickMe (Sri Lanka's Uber). For longer routes, hire a private driver with car (~$60–$90/day). Buses are very cheap but slow. Tuk-tuks are for short hops — always negotiate before getting in.
